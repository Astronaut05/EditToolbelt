# 2026-10-02 · A length the header doesn't really give is measured from the packets

**Decision:** the probe measures a sound or video file's length from its packets (first packet to the end of the last, `packet_span` in `apps/worker/src/etb_worker/probe.py`, up to 5 minutes for a 4 GB upload) when its header gives none, gives less than a millisecond, or belongs to MP3 or ADTS AAC, whose length ffprobe otherwise guesses from the bitrate. A file whose length can't be read even so is refused as `UNSUPPORTED_FORMAT`, and one too slow to measure as `TIMEOUT`. Still images and subtitles are left as they are. Behind it, the worker's cap fails closed: sound or picture whose length reads as nothing is read for a second at most (`priced_seconds`), never in full.
**Why:** the probed length sets the price, the free tier's length limit and how far a job may read (#79, #84). A browser's MediaRecorder WebM has no length in its header, and a header that says 0.1 ms rounded to 0 ms: both priced as nothing and, with no cap for "no length", decoded in full, the free 10 s preview included. ffprobe's bitrate guess for an honest VBR MP3 without a Xing header, or raw AAC, can be far off (57 s and 74 s for two 60 s test files; 90 s for a 300 s MP3 in the review), so the cap cut honest files short with nothing said. Measured, every one is priced and capped by what it holds.
**Reverse:** drop the `_timed` block in `probe_json` and `ESTIMATED`; `priced_seconds` returning `CAP_SLACK_SEC` for an empty length is what keeps such files from being read in full, so keep it.
