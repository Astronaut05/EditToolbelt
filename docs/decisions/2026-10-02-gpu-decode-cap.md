# 2026-10-02 · A GPU call decodes only what its job was priced for

**Decision:**
- **The worker sends the caps.** Every sound or video GPU call carries the most it may decode, from the probe the job was priced and checked on: `max_seconds` = the probed length × 1.02 + 1 s (V20, V21, A12, V17), and for V20 and V21 `max_frames` = the clip's frames + one second's worth, 18,000 at most (`processors/remote.length_cap`, `processors/upscale_video.frame_cap`). The frames are counted at a rate never below the one the GPU function picks (`gpu/video.pick_fps`): the probe's when it's 1 to 120 fps, 30 when it has none, 120 when it's under 1 fps (the function then reads the header's other rate, which the probe doesn't keep). The margin is for honest files whose sound or pictures run a little past the header's length; a test runs the worker's own probe of real MP4, MKV, WebM and variable-rate MOV files through the caps and checks nothing is cut.
- **The decoders stop there.** `-t <max_seconds>` before `-i` on the frame decoder and on the encoder's sound input, `-frames:v <max_frames + 1>`, and a counter in `FramePipe` that hands on `max_frames` and stops the decoder at the next one. Transcription no longer lets Whisper run ffmpeg to the end of the file: `gpu/audio.py` makes Whisper's samples (16 kHz mono) with the same `-t`, and Whisper gets the array.
- **Cut, not refused**, for both the time and the frame caps: the call finishes normally with what was paid for, and the result's notes say the file runs longer than its header says and how much was made. An honest file never reaches a cap, and refusing at the cap would charge nothing for GPU time already spent. The cut is seen as the frame one past the cap (video) or decoding that reached the cap (sound).
- **Checked like the rest of a call's input:** a cap that isn't a positive number (or a whole one, for frames) fails the call with `BAD_INPUT`; one above the function's hard cap is lowered to it; a missing one (a worker from before this) means the hard cap, never "no limit": 18,000 frames and 11 min for the video functions, 4 h 10 min for transcription, the tools' longest paid lengths with the worker's margin (a test holds them to the registry).
- **A file that gives no length is refused** (`NO_DURATION`, credits back) before the GPU: priced at the minimum whatever it holds, it was the same hole without any tampering (a browser's MediaRecorder WebM has no duration in its header). The web shows the worker's sentence for it.

**Why:** the review of the Wave 3 GPU tools: a header that says less than the file holds (an MKV's Segment Duration, a FLAC's STREAMINFO sample count) was priced and limit-checked as a few seconds and then decoded in full. A patched MKV probed at 150 frames made 1,800; on V20 or V21 that runs the model on every frame until Modal's timeout (about $1.20 of L4 a job), past the free tier's 60 s, the 18,000-frame cap and the ProRes check, and holds the whole daily GPU budget.
**Reverse:** drop `max_frames` and `max_seconds` from the processors' options (the functions then use their hard caps), or raise `LENGTH_MARGIN` and `LENGTH_SLACK_SEC` in `processors/remote.py`; for refusing instead of cutting, raise `TOO_LARGE` in `modal_app._video_notes` and the transcribe function where they add the cut note.
