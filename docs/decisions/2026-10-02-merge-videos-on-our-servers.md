# 2026-10-02 · Merge Videos on our servers (M8)

**Decision:**
- **One job takes 2 to 20 clips.** The first clip is the job's own upload (`upload_id`); the others go in the option `clips`, their upload ids in order, 1 to 19, each once (Zod in `@etb/registry/options`).
  - An option in `uploadOptions` may now hold a list. `uploadKinds` says the clips are videos (the four types the tool takes) and `joined`: more of the job's own kind, joined with it. It is Burn Subtitles' second file made general: the job keeps the others in `extra_input_keys` in order, the worker downloads them as `extra-0`, `extra-1`, … and deletes every one when the job ends. No migration.
  - Before anything is charged, each clip must be the caller's own, made for Merge Videos, a video (not a subtitle file), complete, unused by any job, and probed with a picture. A wrong one is named by its place: "Clip 3 has no picture in it, only sound." The same upload named twice is refused before any probe is waited for.
- **Limits are the clips together**, as in the browser: `limits.server` (2 GB and 60 min free, 10 GB and 4 h paid, as Compress Video) holds for their size and length in all; each upload is still checked on its own when it begins, and each clip's pixels. The price is the registry's 1 credit a minute (at least 1) on their probed length together. A tool without joined files keeps its limits checked where they were, before its own refusals. The pure checks are `apps/web/src/server/inputs.ts`, with unit tests.
- **A crossfade longer than half the shortest clip is refused at the quote** (400, "Crossfade too long"), from the probes, so it costs nothing. The worker checks again in frames and fails with `CROSSFADE_TOO_LONG` (credits returned).
- **The fast join on the server is the concat demuxer**, once the worker's own ffprobe of the downloaded clips finds the same video (codec, tag, profile, size, pixel format, aspect, field order, rotation, and the parameter sets by SHA-256) and the same sound, at the same stream positions. The upload probe doesn't record parameter sets. Then:
  - Each clip lasts to the end of its last frame (the list's `duration`), so the next starts there, its own start lined up as the browser lines it up. `-copyts` keeps every packet's time as the list places it.
  - `-auto_convert 0`: the demuxer's automatic Annex B conversion wrote the parameter sets into every H.264 keyframe, so the copy wasn't byte for byte.
  - The browser's `placeCopiedSound` rule, done with the `noise` bitstream filter's `drop` expression by packet number in the joined track: a later clip's encoder priming and any sound packet that starts after or runs more than 1 ms past its picture are left out. The numbers come from ffprobe's packet list of each clip, which the demuxer reads the same way.
  - Uncompressed sound (PCM) that runs past the picture is dropped a packet at a time (the browser cuts it to the sample), so such a join can have a gap of up to one packet. Phones and most cameras record AAC.
  - If ffmpeg refuses the copy, the clips are re-encoded instead, and the notes say so.
- **The result's format is the first clip's, by the type it was uploaded as** (MP4 and MOV look alike to ffprobe; the probe checked the content against the type). The job's input record now carries each file's type (`mime`). A copy whose codecs MP4 or WebM can't hold goes in MKV.
- **Re-encoding follows the browser's plan:** the first clip's size (even) and standard frame rate, or the ones chosen; fitted on black; each clip's sound at 48 kHz stereo, cut or padded to its picture (mono on both sides, past stereo the first two channels); `xfade` (a linear dissolve) and `acrossfade` with quarter-sine curves (equal power). H.264 (CRF 18) with AAC for MP4 and MOV, VP9 (CRF 31) with Opus for WebM and MKV, as the browser picks per format. Rounding halves up, as the browser's `Math.round` does.
  - ffmpeg opens every clip's decoder at the start, so each gets 2 threads: with ffmpeg's default (one per core), 20 HD clips on a 16-core machine ran out of the sandbox's 6 GiB address space; capped, 20 clips at 4K fit.
- **The page:** the shell's combine list counts its files together. Over the browser's limit in all, the server offer shows with that reason; within it, "Use our servers instead" as on Compress Video. The clips go up one after another (an account may have 5 uploads open at once), with the bytes of them all and "File 2 of 5"; a failure or "Not now" deletes every one uploaded. The totals, the offer's reason and the files to send are `joinedFiles` in `ServerNotice.tsx`, which loads only with a server path; the shell keeps a few lines that call it, and `server-run.ts` uploads the clips (`joined` in `serverPath`'s extras).
- **Both paths read the same choices** from `@etb/registry/choices` (no Zod, so the page can import it): transitions, crossfade lengths, sizes, frame rates, 2 to 20 clips.
- **Every input is tried for deletion**, even when one fails: the runner and the scheduler record which went, and the job keeps the keys of any left for the sweeper (within the hour). Before, a failure on one key left every later one to the sweeper.
- **Each clip still waits at most an hour** after its upload finishes. A set that takes longer than an hour to upload loses its first clips ("Upload expired", nothing charged). The one-hour rule for user files is a promise; at 100 Mbit/s, 10 GB goes up in about 15 minutes.
- **The server switch stays off** (Admin → Tools → Merge Videos → "Server path on"), as Compress Video's did. The worker's disk holds a copy join's clips and its result at once: about twice the clips' size per job slot.

**Why:** `tools/video.md` → V12 ("large totals → server"), [2026-10-01-merge-videos-m8.md](2026-10-01-merge-videos-m8.md), and rule 1 (the browser stays the default).
**Reverse:** switch the path off in Admin; `apps/worker/src/etb_worker/processors/merge_videos.py`; `mergeVideos` and its `uploadKinds` entry in `packages/registry/src/options.ts`; `apps/web/src/server/inputs.ts`; `joined` in `serverPath`'s extras.
