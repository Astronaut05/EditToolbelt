# 2026-11-13 · The worker's review fixes: what a job may cost, and what outlives it

**Decision:** seven findings from the review of 2026-11-12 are fixed in the worker. The calls the spec didn't settle:

- **A video's frame rate is checked against its packets.** A file's header can say 60 fps over 1,000 fps of frames (Matroska's `DefaultDuration` is the uploader's to write), and the job then decodes many times what it was priced for, times out and is refunded. The probe now counts the picture's packets over the whole file, in the pass that measures the length where there is one. The header's rate stands while the packets fit it: no more than the header's rate over the length plus 2 %, plus a lead-in of 10 s at that rate (250 frames at least). Past that, `video.fps` is packets over length, and `video.header_fps` keeps the header's rate for the GPU frame cap. A plain maximum would misread honest files: 241 frames at 240 fps read as 240.04, and a clip cut without re-encoding carries the packets back to its last keyframe (a GOP is 250 frames by x264's default, 10 s by HandBrake's).
- **ffprobe is asked only for the fields the worker reads, and its answer is kept up to 2 MiB.** Asked for everything it prints every tag in the file, and 12 tags of 8 MiB made the worker hold 300 MB outside the sandbox. A longer answer, or a line over 64 KiB, refuses the file. CI's stack job runs the same calls inside the worker image, whose ffmpeg (Debian's 7.x) is newer than the tests' (`apps/worker/scripts/check_image_probe.py`).
- **The time limit is the job's.** `limits.timeoutSec` applied to each ffmpeg run afresh, so Compress Video's two passes and their retry could hold a slot for four times the limit, and the download and upload had none. One deadline is set as the job starts; each run gets what is left; nothing is uploaded past it. Compress Video keeps its first result, with its "just over the target" note, when the limit leaves less than 1.25 times the first encode for the second try. The limits in the registry (Compress Video, Merge Videos, Burn Subtitles and VFR to CFR 2 h, Noise Reduction 1 h) were set per run and are now per job: a job at the very top of a tool's input limits that used to finish in several full-length runs now ends as a timeout with its credits back.
- **An ended job's input is found from its row.** It could stay in storage for up to an hour when the job was cancelled while its worker was dead, or when a tick or a runner was killed between marking the job and deleting the input. Every tick the scheduler reads ended jobs that still hold an input and whose heartbeat is over a minute old, and deletes under a lock on the job's row, so an admin's retry either keeps the input or finds it gone. `jobs_inputs_idx` (migration 0013) serves that read.
- **Uploads are probed by a thread of their own.** Only CPU slots probed, between jobs: with both inside a two-hour job no upload could be quoted, for any tool. The slots still probe between jobs too.
- **An alert no channel took is tried again.** It was recorded as sent and the once-only rules never raised it again, an uncredited payment included. It is kept as `undelivered` in `alerts.channels` (no new column) and sent again each tick, oldest try first, with at most 20 s a tick spent on sends that fail. The digest is tried again every 10 minutes.
- **Worker connections have limits.** 30 s for a statement, 5 s for a lock, 5 minutes for the nightly jobs that read whole tables, and a host that vanished is noticed in about a minute (the kernel's default is two hours). A held row used to stop the scheduler's tick, which holds the leader lock, on every worker. A finished job is recorded with three tries, so a row held for a moment doesn't fail an hour's work.

**Left open:**
- An upload whose probe kills the worker is still probed again without limit. The known way to kill it (ffprobe's answer in memory) is closed; a retry limit needs a column (`uploads.probe_attempts`).
- A transfer to or from storage already under way isn't stopped at the job's deadline; boto3's own timeouts bound it.
- The dead-host window (about 60 s) equals the reaper's, so a job can still be requeued while its first run carries on for a few seconds. Shorter keepalives would close it.
- The retention sweep's two reads of `uploads` are table scans, once per 5 minutes.

**Why:** the first three let one upload cost far more than it paid for; the fourth broke the rule that an input goes the moment its job ends; the last three could stop all work, or hide that something had, without anyone being told.

**Reverse:** `_frame_rate` returning the header's rate; `-show_format -show_streams` and a plain list as the collector; `JobContext.deadline` left as None; the scheduler's `_drop_inputs` back to the reaped and expired lists; `run_probes` out of `slot_threads`; `UNDELIVERED` rows counted as sent; `connect_url` without its options. The index is dropped with a new migration.
