# 2026-10-02 · The worker is watched from outside, every 30 minutes

**Decision:** two changes so a stopped worker or sweeper can't go unnoticed (final worker review, S1):
- **The scheduler loop outlives any failure.** `Scheduler.tick` catches everything, logs the error's type only, and runs each step (queue upkeep and the sweep, alerts, daily jobs) even when one before it failed. `main.py` guards the loop once more, and if the loop ever ends without a stop signal the slots stop too, so the process exits and Railway restarts it.
- **An outside check.** The web answers `GET /readyz/worker`: the age in seconds of the freshest worker heartbeat (stale after 10 min) and of the sweeper's last finished pass (stale after 30 min), 200 or 503 problem+json. A new scheduled workflow, **Watch** (`.github/workflows/watch.yml`, at :13 and :43), reads it through Cloudflare Access with CI's existing service token (`scripts/ops/verify.ts` → `worker`, only run when named) and fails when it isn't 200; GitHub emails about a failed scheduled run. It skips with a notice until `CF_ACCESS_CLIENT_ID`, `CF_ACCESS_CLIENT_SECRET` and the `SITE_URL` variable are set, like Smoke. No new secret.

**Why:** every alert rule runs inside the worker, so a worker that is down, crash-looping or stuck without its scheduler sends nothing, and nothing else looked. Both numbers live only in the database, which only the web can read from outside. A separate path rather than `/readyz`: `/readyz` is Railway's health check and skips Access, so making it depend on the worker would block the web's deploys whenever the worker is down, and would show worker details to anyone who reaches Railway's own hostname. `/readyz/worker` goes through Access (`src/server/access.ts` exempts only exact paths) and says only two ages. 10 minutes for the heartbeat (the worker alerts at 2) rides out a deploy's restart; 30 minutes for the sweeper matches `sweeper_stale`. Every 30 minutes costs nothing on a public repository and keeps a dead worker's files to well under the backstop's day. Its own workflow rather than a schedule on Ops: Ops runs every check whose secrets are set (an R2 round trip, Cloudflare's API) and its manual job check can hold its concurrency group for an hour.

At Go public, when Access comes off the whole domain, `/readyz/worker` needs a guard of its own (keep an Access application on it, as on `/admin`) or it becomes readable by anyone; it says nothing private, only two ages.

**Reverse:** delete `.github/workflows/watch.yml`, `apps/web/src/app/readyz/worker/` and `apps/web/src/server/worker-health.ts`, and the `worker` check in `scripts/ops/verify.ts`. The scheduler's catch-alls can stay either way.
